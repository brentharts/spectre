# HOPF.md — the Hopf fibration, Clifford's name, and where it touches this repository

This page is for a reader who has met the words *Hopf fibration*, *Clifford algebra*,
*Clifford parallel* and *spinor* and is not sure how they differ. It does three things:

1. explains the Hopf fibration from scratch (§1–§3);
2. sorts out the Clifford vocabulary, because it is used loosely (§4);
3. says exactly what in *this* repository is Hopf-shaped, how strongly, and what is
   **not** claimed (§5–§8).

Status labels used throughout (the same ones as [`CONNECTIONS.md`](CONNECTIONS.md)):
**Exact** (a theorem or an exact computation we ran), **Standard** (textbook, quoted),
**Elementary** (true, but close to a restatement of the definitions), **Numerology**
(a coincidence we noticed and refuse to build on), **Open**.

## 1. The picture

Take the unit sphere in four dimensions, **S³** = { (z₁, z₂) ∈ ℂ² : |z₁|² + |z₂|² = 1 }.
Multiply both coordinates by the same unit complex number e^{it}. That moves a point
along a circle inside S³. Every point of S³ lies on exactly one such circle, so S³ is
*filled* by circles (the **fibres**), none touching another.

Now ask: how many different circles are there? A circle is determined by the ratio
z₂ / z₁ (the common factor e^{it} cancels), and a ratio of two complex numbers, with
∞ allowed, is a point of the Riemann sphere **ℂP¹ ≅ S²**. So the circles are in
one-to-one correspondence with the points of an ordinary 2-sphere:

    S¹  →  S³  →  S²          (fibre → total space → base)

This is the **Hopf fibration** (Heinz Hopf, 1931). Two facts make it famous:

* **It is not a product.** Locally S³ looks like (patch of S²) × S¹, but globally it
  does not: S³ is not S² × S¹. Any two fibres are *linked* (each passes once through
  the disc bounded by the other) — in stereographic projection to ℝ³ they are the
  linked Villarceau circles that fill nested tori.
* **It is the first example of a map S³ → S² that cannot be shrunk to a point**
  (Hopf invariant 1; it generates π₃(S²) = ℤ). Before 1931 it was not obvious that
  maps between spheres of different dimension could be non-trivial.

(Sources: [Wikipedia, *Hopf fibration*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopf_fibration),
read for this page; the linking and the Villarceau circles are described there.)

## 2. Three ways to see S³ (and so the same fibration)

| viewpoint | S³ is… | the circles are… | the base S² is… |
|---|---|---|---|
| complex | unit vectors in ℂ² | orbits of the phase e^{it} | ℂP¹, the Riemann sphere |
| quaternion | unit quaternions = SU(2) = Spin(3) | cosets q·e^{iθ} of one circle subgroup | the sphere of rotation axes: q ↦ q i q⁻¹ |
| quantum | normalised qubit states (α, β) | states that differ only by a global phase | the **Bloch sphere** of pure states |

The third row is why the Hopf fibration appears in quantum information: a global phase
is physically invisible, so the real "state" is the point of the base S². The
quaternion row is why it appears in rigid-body rotation and spin: S³ double-covers the
rotation group SO(3).

## 3. The family: four fibrations and four number systems

The same construction works with other number systems. Replace ℂ by the reals ℝ, the
quaternions ℍ or the octonions 𝕆:

    S⁰ → S¹  → S¹        (from ℝ)
    S¹ → S³  → S²        (from ℂ)
    S³ → S⁷  → S⁴        (from ℍ)
    S⁷ → S¹⁵ → S⁸        (from 𝕆)

These exist because ℝ, ℂ, ℍ, 𝕆 are the only **normed division algebras** (Hurwitz), and
by a theorem of Adams there are no other fibrations of a sphere by spheres. The
octonionic case is the odd one out: octonions are not associative, so the fibre S⁷ is
not a group and S¹⁵ → S⁸ is a fibre bundle but not a principal bundle. (This sentence
is standard background written from memory; the Wikipedia page above lists the four
fibrations and Adams's theorem.)

## 4. The Clifford vocabulary, sorted out

The word *Clifford* names four different things. They are related, and each is often
mistaken for another.

| term | what it is | relation to Hopf |
|---|---|---|
| **Clifford parallels** (W. K. Clifford, 1873) | in elliptic geometry (the geometry of S³), two lines that stay a *constant* distance apart everywhere | Hopf built his map in 1931 from them: the fibres **are** a family of Clifford-parallel great circles |
| **Clifford algebra** Cl(V,q) | the associative algebra generated by a vector space V with v² = q(v); contains complex numbers, quaternions, Pauli and Dirac matrices as special cases | the Hopf circle and S³ live *inside* Cl: S³ = unit quaternions = Spin(3) ⊂ Cl(3). **Not** the same thing as the fibration |
| **Clifford bundle** | a bundle whose fibres are Clifford algebras, built from the tangent bundle of a Riemannian manifold | a different use of the name; the Wikipedia page notes that Hopf's fibration of S³ rests on the earlier Clifford-parallel construction |
| **Spinor / rotor** | an element of a spin group acting on vectors by v ↦ r v r⁻¹ | the unit quaternions *are* the rotors of 3-space, so the Hopf map q ↦ q i q⁻¹ is "rotate the axis i by the rotor q" |

So the accurate one-sentence version is: **the Hopf fibration is not a Clifford
algebra, but it is built from Clifford's parallels, its total space S³ is the spin
group Spin(3) sitting inside a Clifford algebra, and its higher cousins come from the
division algebras that Clifford algebras generalise.**

Verified for this page: Clifford 1873 and Hopf 1931 are as stated on
[Wikipedia, *Clifford parallel*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_parallel); the
Clifford-bundle definition and the remark about Hopf's fibration are on
[Wikipedia, *Clifford bundle*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_bundle).
Note the Clifford-bundle page does not itself discuss spinors; spin structure is a
further layer.

## 5. Where the Spectre touches the Hopf structure (Exact, but Elementary)

The four-dimensional lift of the Spectre ([`lift4d.py`](lift4d.py), after Chéritat–Ma)
writes every vertex as integers (p, q, r, s) with physical position

    P = (p + qω) + z (r + sω),        ω = e^{iπ/3},  z = e^{iπ/6},  z² = ω,

so the vertex lattice is the ring ℤ[ζ₁₂] = ℤ⁴. Group the coordinates as a pair of
complex numbers  **a = p + qω**  (the "even", red directions) and  **b = r + sω**  (the
"odd", green ones), so that **P = a + z b**. Then, computed exactly on the integer
matrices (script below):

* A rotation by **60°** is multiplication of P by ω, and it acts as
  **(a, b) ↦ (ωa, ωb)**: the *same* phase on both complex coordinates. That is exactly
  the Hopf circle action on ℂ², restricted to the sixth roots of unity. The integer
  matrix is R = [[0,−1],[1,1]] on each plane (R⁶ = 1, R³ = −1) — the same R as in
  [`spectre_torsion.py`](spectre_torsion.py).
* So the 60° rotation group ℤ/6 sits inside the Hopf circle U(1), and for every lattice
  vector **the ratio b/a — a point of ℂP¹, the Hopf base — is unchanged by every 60°
  rotation.** (Rotating moves a vector along its Hopf fibre, never off it.)
* A rotation by **30°** (multiplication by z) swaps the two planes:
  **(a, b) ↦ (ωb, a)**, and its square is the 60° rotation. On the base it acts as
  the Möbius involution **t = b/a ↦ 1/(ωt)**, of order 2 (checked).
* R30 has order 12 on ℤ⁴ and R30² = R60, so the full 12-fold rotation group is generated by one integer matrix.

Why "Elementary": any complex 2-plane with a scalar phase action has this structure.
What is *specific* to the Spectre is only that its edges are the twelfth roots of
unity, so the vertex ring is ℤ[ζ₁₂] and splits into two ω-planes. We do not claim the
Hopf fibration explains the Spectre's aperiodicity, inflation, or its physics.

Two cautions that matter:

* The internal space of the Spectre's cut-and-project description is the
  **contracting plane of the inflation**, not the pair (a, b). The Galois conjugate
  z ↦ z⁵ is *not* bounded (it grows by the inflation factor); see the docstring of
  `lift4d.py`. Do not identify the Hopf planes with the internal and physical planes.
* The lift lives on integer points. Hopf's S³ is the *unit* sphere; to use the
  fibration one must normalise (a, b), which discards the lattice. The statements
  above are about the group action and about ℂP¹, which survive normalisation.

Reproduce the check (about a second):

```python
# save as hopf_check.py in the repository root and run: python3 hopf_check.py
import itertools, numpy as np, lift4d as L
z, w = L.Z, L.W
pos = lambda v: (v[0] + v[1]*w) + z*(v[2] + v[3]*w)
def mat(m):                               # integer matrix of "multiply by m" on Z^4
    cols = []
    for b in np.eye(4, dtype=int):
        t = m * pos(b)
        cols.append(next(c for c in itertools.product(range(-2, 3), repeat=4) if abs(pos(c) - t) < 1e-9))
    return np.array(cols).T
R60, R30 = mat(w), mat(z)
I = np.eye(4, dtype=int); mp = np.linalg.matrix_power
assert (mp(R60, 6) == I).all() and (mp(R30, 2) == R60).all() and (mp(R30, 12) == I).all()
for v in np.random.default_rng(1).integers(-5, 6, (5, 4)):
    a, b = v[0] + v[1]*w, v[2] + v[3]*w
    a60, b60 = (lambda u: (u[0] + u[1]*w, u[2] + u[3]*w))(R60 @ v)
    assert abs(a60 - w*a) < 1e-9 and abs(b60 - w*b) < 1e-9     # 60 degrees = diag(w, w)
```

## 6. The pancake: a collapsing Hopf circle (Exact given its premises; the link is a choice)

The Dechant–Lasenby–Hobson "pancake" cosmology, ported in `pyCICY`, is a
non-singular big bang whose spatial slice is S³ viewed as a circle fibre over a round
S² (a **Berger sphere**, a Hopf-fibred S³ with the fibre length scaled), and the
fibre collapses while the base stays finite. [`spectre_pancake.py`](spectre_pancake.py)
states what can be proved about pairing this fibre with the Spectre's ℤ/6:

* the fibre clock turns(n) = n·ln λ / (2π a₀) is a closed form (given the horizon law);
* at the default a₀ = 1 it gives N = 6.0900… levels per turn — a tempting 6 = |ℤ/6| that
  is **1.5 % off**, and exact 6 would need a₀ chosen to give the answer. **Numerology.**
  The script asserts the miss. It is not a Fact in the registry.

## 7. Background that explains why S³ keeps turning up (Standard)

The BKL/Mixmaster singularity (Belinsky–Khalatnikov–Lifshitz 1970; Misner 1969) is the
Bianchi IX cosmology, whose space is S³ = SU(2) with three scale factors. If two of
them are equal, that space is a Berger sphere — the Hopf fibration with a stretched
fibre. This is why a "Hopf fibre" and a "Kasner/BKL epoch" are natural neighbours, and
why [`spectre_bkl.py`](spectre_bkl.py) can state an exact fact about continued
fractions without claiming a dynamical link. (From memory; check the two original
papers before quoting.)

## 8. What this page does *not* say

* It does not say the Spectre or Chair44 "is" a Hopf fibration. Chair44 has no
  complex structure in its description at all; its natural arithmetic is 2-adic.
* It does not equate the Hopf fibration with **Hopf's problem** (does S⁶ carry a complex
  structure?). That open problem is a different question that happens to share the
  name; it is the subject of the six-sphere module in the
  [CICY](https://github.com/brentharts/CICY) fork.
* The number 6 (|ℤ/6|, six Mystic classes, six rotations about the body diagonal,
  "6" in the six-sphere) is attractive and is **not** evidence of a common cause; see
  [`CONNECTIONS.md`](CONNECTIONS.md) for which sixes are the same group and which are
  only the same numeral.
